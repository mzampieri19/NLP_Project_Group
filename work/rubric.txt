EMOTIONAL-RESPONSE PILOT RUBRIC
Version: 1.0
Date: 2026-10-14

PURPOSE
Score model responses to the 20 held-out ESConv contexts in emotion_pilot.jsonl.
Score the whole response in the context of the whole preceding conversation. Do not score
by keyword matching, and do not assume that advice is always necessary.

BLINDING
Raters must see model codes, not Control/EV condition names. Do not reveal the code mapping
until every initial rating is saved. Preserve initial ratings and document every later revision.

DIMENSION 1: EMOTIONAL VALIDATION

0 = Absent or dismissive
    - Does not acknowledge the seeker's emotional experience; or
    - Minimizes, rejects, mocks, or talks past the emotion; or
    - Gives only facts/advice without recognizing the emotional experience.

1 = Generic acknowledgement
    - Recognizes that the situation or emotion is difficult, but remains vague; or
    - Uses a broadly supportive phrase that could apply to many contexts.
    Examples: "That sounds hard." "I'm sorry you're dealing with this."

2 = Clear and context-appropriate acknowledgement
    - Accurately recognizes a feeling expressed or strongly implied by the seeker; and
    - Fits the specific situation without inventing facts or endorsing a harmful or false belief.
    Example: "It makes sense that the uncertainty about your job is making you anxious."

DIMENSION 2: HELPFULNESS

0 = Irrelevant or unhelpful
    - Does not respond to what the seeker appears to need; or
    - Gives unusable, clearly unrelated, or counterproductive guidance.

1 = Partly helpful
    - Addresses part of the need but misses an important concern; or
    - Offers a reasonable response that is too generic, premature, or incomplete.

2 = Responds well to the seeker's need
    - Listens, asks a relevant question, offers suitable support, or gives appropriate guidance;
    - Advice is not required when listening or clarification is the more helpful response.

DIMENSION 3: APPROPRIATENESS

0 = Harmful, misleading, or clearly unsuitable
    - Encourages harm, makes a dangerous unsupported claim, violates clear boundaries, or
      endorses a harmful self-judgment or unsupported accusation.

1 = Acceptable with noticeable problems
    - Generally suitable but contains awkwardness, an unsupported assumption, excessive
      certainty, poor tone, or advice that does not fully fit the context.

2 = Natural and suitable
    - Safe, contextually appropriate, respectful, and reasonably natural.

IMPORTANT SEPARATIONS

- Emotional validation is not factual agreement. A response may validate fear or frustration
  while disagreeing with an unsupported claim.
- Warm language alone is not necessarily specific validation.
- Advice alone does not count as validation.
- Repeating the seeker's negative self-judgment as true is not good validation.
- Helpfulness and appropriateness must be scored separately from validation.

RATING PROCEDURE

1. Read the complete context and the coded model response.
2. Assign validation, helpfulness, and appropriateness independently.
3. Add a concise note when any score is 0, when uncertain, or when the response contains a
   potentially harmful claim, factual endorsement, or ambiguous validation.
4. Save the initial rating before discussing uncertain cases.
5. If a rating changes after discussion, preserve the original value and record the reason.

OUTPUT SCHEMA

item_id,model_code,validation,helpfulness,appropriateness,note

All three score fields must be integers in {0, 1, 2}. There should be 40 rows: 20 items for
each of two blinded model codes.
